**Document Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** February 11, 2026  
**Status:** Active  
**Classification:** Internal Use

---

## 1. Executive Summary

This Charter establishes the formal authorization and framework for AI initiatives under the SEVEN-G methodology. It defines the strategic intent, governance structure, resource allocation, and success criteria necessary to guide the initiative from conception through implementation and value realization.

**Key Highlights:**
- **Purpose:** Transform business operations through strategic AI adoption aligned with organizational capabilities and maturity
- **Approach:** Systematic, risk-aware implementation following SEVEN-G principles (Strategy, Exploration, Validation, Execution, Navigation, Governance)
- **Expected Outcome:** Measurable business value through AI-driven innovation, efficiency, and competitive advantage
- **Duration:** Typically 6-24 months depending on scope and complexity
- **Investment Level:** To be defined per specific initiative (see Section 7)

This Charter serves as the authoritative reference for all stakeholders and provides the mandate for the initiative team to proceed with planning and execution.

## 2. Initiative Overview

### 2.1 Purpose and Objectives

**Primary Purpose:**
To systematically assess, design, implement, and operationalize AI capabilities that deliver measurable business value while managing risks and ensuring organizational alignment.

**Strategic Objectives:**

1. **Business Value Creation**
   - Increase operational efficiency by 20-40%
   - Enable new revenue streams through AI-powered products/services
   - Reduce costs in targeted business processes
   - Improve decision-making quality and speed

2. **Capability Building**
   - Develop organizational AI maturity from current baseline to target level
   - Build internal expertise and knowledge capital
   - Establish repeatable AI development and deployment processes
   - Create a culture of data-driven innovation

3. **Risk Management**
   - Ensure ethical AI deployment aligned with regulations and principles
   - Maintain data security, privacy, and compliance
   - Implement robust governance and monitoring mechanisms
   - Establish clear accountability and oversight

4. **Competitive Positioning**
   - Maintain/achieve market leadership through AI innovation
   - Respond to competitive threats with advanced capabilities
   - Create defensible competitive advantages
   - Enable future scalability and adaptation

**Example Objective Statement:**
> "To implement a customer service AI assistant that reduces response time by 60%, improves customer satisfaction scores by 25%, and handles 70% of tier-1 inquiries autonomously within 12 months, while maintaining data privacy compliance and agent job quality."

## 5. Success Criteria and KPIs

### Primary Success Criteria

**Business Value Metrics:**

1. **Financial Impact**
   - ROI: Target ≥ 200% within 24 months
   - Cost savings: $[X]M annually
   - Revenue growth: $[Y]M annually
   - Payback period: ≤ 18 months

2. **Operational Efficiency**
   - Process time reduction: ≥ 30%
   - Error rate reduction: ≥ 50%
   - Throughput increase: ≥ 25%
   - Resource utilization improvement: ≥ 20%

3. **Customer Impact**
   - Customer satisfaction (CSAT): +15 points
   - Net Promoter Score (NPS): +10 points
   - Customer effort score: -30%
   - Resolution time: -40%

4. **Quality Improvements**
   - Defect rate reduction: ≥ 40%
   - Prediction accuracy: ≥ 85%
   - Decision quality improvement: ≥ 30%

**Technical Performance Metrics:**

| Metric | Target | Measurement Method |
|--------|--------|-------------------|
| Model Accuracy | ≥ 90% | Test set evaluation |
| Model Precision | ≥ 85% | Confusion matrix analysis |
| Model Recall | ≥ 85% | Confusion matrix analysis |
| Response Time | ≤ 500ms (p95) | Application monitoring |
| System Availability | ≥ 99.9% | Uptime monitoring |
| Data Freshness | ≤ 1 hour lag | Pipeline monitoring |
| API Success Rate | ≥ 99.5% | API gateway metrics |

**Adoption & Change Metrics:**

| Metric | Target | Timeline |
|--------|--------|----------|
| User Adoption Rate | ≥ 80% | 6 months post-launch |
| Active Users (DAU/MAU) | ≥ 70% | Ongoing |
| Training Completion | 100% required users | 3 months post-launch |
| User Satisfaction | ≥ 4.0/5.0 | Ongoing |
| Support Ticket Volume | ≤ 5% of users/month | 3 months post-launch |

**Project Execution Metrics:**

- Schedule variance: ≤ 10%
- Budget variance: ≤ 10%
- Scope creep: ≤ 15% approved changes
- Risk materialization: ≤ 20% of identified risks
- Defect density: ≤ 2 critical defects per 1000 function points

### Definition of Done

The initiative is considered successfully complete when:

1. ✓ All primary deliverables are accepted by sponsors
2. ✓ Production system is live and stable for 30 consecutive days
3. ✓ Target KPIs achieved or trajectory clearly established
4. ✓ All users trained and support structures in place
5. ✓ Operations team fully capable of BAU management
6. ✓ Benefits realization plan executed and tracked
7. ✓ Lessons learned documented and shared
8. ✓ Final governance review completed and approved

### Measurement & Reporting

**Measurement Approach:**
- Baseline measurements captured pre-implementation
- Weekly operational metrics monitoring
- Monthly business KPI reporting
- Quarterly strategic review with sponsors
- Post-implementation review at 6, 12, and 24 months

**Reporting Structure:**
- **Weekly:** Operational dashboard for team
- **Bi-weekly:** Status report to steering committee
- **Monthly:** Executive summary to sponsors
- **Quarterly:** Board/leadership presentation

## 9. Governance Structure

### Governance Framework

**Three-Tier Governance Model:**

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│   Tier 1: Strategic Steering Committee   │
│   (Monthly - Strategic Direction)        │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘
                    │
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│   Tier 2: Program Management Office      │
│   (Weekly - Operational Oversight)       │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘
                    │
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│   Tier 3: Execution Teams                │
│   (Daily - Delivery & Implementation)    │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Tier 1: Strategic Steering Committee

**Purpose:** Strategic direction, major decisions, issue escalation resolution

**Membership:**
- Executive Sponsor (Chair)
- Business Sponsor
- CTO/CIO
- Chief Data Officer
- CFO or Finance Representative
- CISO or Security Representative
- Program Manager (non-voting)

**Meeting Cadence:** Monthly (or ad-hoc for critical decisions)

**Responsibilities:**
- Approve charter and major scope changes
- Resolve strategic conflicts and blockers
- Approve budget reallocations >$100K
- Review progress against KPIs
- Make GO/NO-GO decisions at gates
- Provide executive sponsorship and escalation support

**Decision Authority:**
- Strategic direction and priorities
- Scope changes >15% impact
- Budget changes >10%
- Schedule changes >1 month
- Risk acceptance for high/critical risks
- Resource allocation for senior roles

### Tier 2: Program Management Office

**Purpose:** Day-to-day oversight, coordination, risk management

**Membership:**
- Program Manager (Chair)
- Technical Lead
- Data Science Lead
- Business Analyst Lead
- Change Management Lead
- Representatives from key stakeholder groups

**Meeting Cadence:** Weekly

**Responsibilities:**
- Monitor progress against plan
- Manage risks, issues, and dependencies
- Coordinate across workstreams
- Review and manage change requests
- Ensure quality and compliance
- Prepare steering committee materials
- Manage budget within approved limits

**Decision Authority:**
- Tactical implementation decisions
- Scope changes <15% impact
- Budget variances within tolerance
- Resource allocation (non-senior)
- Risk mitigation approaches

### Tier 3: Execution Teams

**Purpose:** Delivery of specific workstreams and components

**Teams:**
- Data Engineering Team
- ML/AI Development Team
- Integration & Development Team
- Testing & QA Team
- Change Management Team

**Meeting Cadence:** Daily standups, weekly team syncs

**Responsibilities:**
- Execute assigned work packages
- Track and report progress
- Identify and flag issues early
- Deliver quality outputs on schedule
- Collaborate across teams

### Decision-Making Framework

**Decision Types and Authority:**

| Decision Type | Authority | Approval Process |
|--------------|-----------|------------------|
| Strategic direction | Steering Committee | Formal vote, documented |
| Major scope change | Steering Committee | Business case, impact analysis |
| Architecture decisions | Technical Lead + Architect Review | Documented ADR (Architecture Decision Record) |
| Technology selection | Technical Lead | Options analysis, approval |
| Budget reallocation <10% | Program Manager | Notification to steering |
| Budget reallocation >10% | Steering Committee | Formal approval required |
| Risk mitigation approach | PMO | Documented in risk register |
| Risk acceptance (high/critical) | Steering Committee | Formal acceptance sign-off |
| Schedule changes <2 weeks | PMO | Impact assessment |
| Schedule changes >2 weeks | Steering Committee | Revised plan approval |

### Escalation Process

**Escalation Levels:**

1. **Level 1 - Team Lead:** Issues within team scope
   - Response time: 24 hours
   - Examples: Technical problems, resource conflicts within team

2. **Level 2 - Program Manager:** Cross-team issues, budget/schedule risks
   - Response time: 48 hours
   - Examples: Inter-team dependencies, moderate risks

3. **Level 3 - PMO / Steering:** Strategic issues, major risks/blockers
   - Response time: 1 week (or emergency meeting)
   - Examples: Major scope changes, critical risks, budget overruns

4. **Level 4 - Executive Sponsor:** Organization-level blockers
   - Response time: As needed
   - Examples: Resource unavailability, strategic conflicts

**Escalation Triggers:**
- Issue unresolved within 5 business days at current level
- Impact exceeds decision authority at current level
- Risk score increases to high or critical
- Budget variance exceeds tolerance
- Schedule slip exceeds tolerance
- Stakeholder conflicts unresolved
