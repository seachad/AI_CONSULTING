## 1. Objective

By the end you will be able to place any AI initiative in a sphere and an ambition level, and you will understand why that classification changes the criteria by which it is judged.

---

## 2. Key ideas

1. **Nine spheres.** Seven where value is created or enabled (customer, product and service, people, operations, data, knowledge, decision), one of boundaries (regulation, ethics and accountability) and one meta-sphere (AI governance).
2. **Three ambition levels**: *Optimise* (doing the same better), *Augment* (doing more than was possible) and *Transform* (doing something that was not possible before, or changing the model).
3. **Classification has consequences.** Transform always implies Enterprise intensity and board approval; in exchange, it is not required to deliver the immediate return of an efficiency project.
4. **Classification follows rules, not enthusiasm.** Ambition is proposed in phase 1, confirmed in phase 2 and checked against what happens in production.

## 4. Exercise

In the example register (T01), open the record of IA-2026-002 (predictive maintenance as a service for industrial customers), classified as Transform, and that of IA-2025-001 (invoice classification), classified as Optimise. Explain in two sentences why each has that level. Then classify three real initiatives from your company.

2. Can an initiative have two primary spheres?
3. Who approves a Transform bet and at which gates?
4. Where is the value of a platform used by several cases allocated?
