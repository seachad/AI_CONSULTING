References to general regulation (such as the EU AI Act, the GDPR, DORA or NIS2), to technical standards and to regulation specific to each sector or jurisdiction may be incomplete, may not apply to a particular case or may become out of date as a result of regulatory changes, interpretations or supervisory positions after their consultation date. **Each organisation that uses SEVEN-G is solely responsible for identifying the regulation that applies to it, verifying that it is current and certifying its own regulatory compliance**, with the appropriate qualified advice. The author accepts no liability for any use made of this content or for any decisions taken on the basis of it. The data, figures, companies and cases in the examples are fictitious or illustrative.

> **Why it matters.** A poorly defined engagement produces deliverables that nobody can accept, incompatibilities discovered too late and promises the framework does not allow. This template requires the support model to be chosen, what is out of scope to be fixed, independence to be checked before signing, the SEVEN-G licence and legal notice to be passed on, and agreement from the outset on how each deliverable will be accepted and how the third party will exit. It protects the company, which decides and keeps its data, and the third party, which is responsible only for its service.

> **This template does not constitute legal advice.** The clauses are indicative; the company and the third party must adapt them to their applicable law, their general terms and their procurement policies with the appropriate qualified advice.

## 1. Instructions for use

| Aspect | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **When it is used** | Before starting any service in which a third party applies SEVEN-G in the company, and whenever the model, scope or team changes. |
| **Who completes it** | The third party (consultancy, audit firm, independent adviser or team from another group company) drafts the proposal; the company completes its roles, prerequisites and data. |
| **Who verifies it** | A quality reviewer from the third party not involved in the proposal (91 §7.1, commitment 5); for the company, whoever commissions the work and, for the clauses, its legal counsel. |
| **Who decides or approves** | Senior management commissioning the work (M1, M2, M5); board committee or internal audit (M3); chair of the board or of the board committee (M4). |
| **Stage or point** | M1: C1 · M2: C1 to C3 and phase 0 of priority initiatives · M3: verification of 01 §14 · M4: C2, C4 and C5 · M5: C3, C4 and lifecycle (91 §4.1). |
| **Tool** | It has no tool of its own. Deliverables use T01, T02, T14, T15, T16, T17, T18 and T19 depending on the model. |
| **Reference document** | Document 91 (models, independence, use of the name, quality, what must not be promised); document 93 (licence, citation and notice); document 90 (90-day plan and prerequisites). |
| **Lite versus Enterprise** | The company's Lite or Enterprise implementation scope (90 §2) is stated in section 2 and adjusts the calendar and the deliverables. |

Completion rules:

- Data that depend on each engagement are shown in **[square brackets]** and are replaced when the template is completed.
- **One model per engagement.** If several are combined, their compatibility is checked in section 7 and each is described separately.
- **Commercial terms are free** (91 §1); this template only requires the fee structure to be declared and 91 §5.4 to be respected.
- **No promises** of certification, guaranteed compliance, savings percentages, maturity levels or timeframes without a diagnosis (91 §8).
