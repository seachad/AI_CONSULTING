Risk and regulation

What risk is accepted and which rule applies to each system.

In brief

What this area is about

Every AI system is inventoried and classified under the regulation that applies to it. Its risks are assessed before and after controls, and the remaining risk is accepted by the body that matches its level. Incidents and non-conformities are recorded and corrected within a deadline.

Concepts

The concepts you need

Definitions taken from the SEVEN-G glossary (document 02). The codes are links to where each one is explained.

Inherent risk
Level of risk without considering the use case's specific controls.
Residual risk
Level of risk with controls. Target: expected after applying the planned controls (G3 and G4). Verified: after checking the effectiveness of the controls with evidence (G5 and R6).
Risk acceptance
Recorded decision, with validity period and conditions, whereby the competent level assumes a residual risk within the risk appetite. Low: product owner · Medium: sponsor with risk clearance from the risk owner · High: AI Committee · Critical: not accepted; exceptionally, only the board or its board committee. It lapses if the level, system, supplier, model, regulatory classification or autonomy changes.
Regulatory classification
Determination, with qualified legal judgement, of the system's category under the applicable regulation (Prohibited, High risk, Transparency obligations, Minimal risk, Out of scope, Pending classification) and of the company's role. It is provisional in phase 0 and final in phase 3.
High-risk AI system
System classified as high-risk under the EU AI Act: safety component or product under Annex I, or an Annex III area without the Article 6(3) exception. It is an Enterprise criterion. The application dates of its obligations are dealt with in document 34.
EU AI Act
Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, as amended. Its mapping to phases, roles and evidence, and the status of its application dates, are set out in document 34.
Nonconformity
Failure to meet a mandatory requirement of the framework ("must"), classified as minor, major or critical, and managed with containment, root cause, corrective action, effectiveness verification, closure and re-audit.
AI incident
Event in which an AI system, through its operation, failure, manipulation or use, causes or may cause harm to people, to the company or to third parties, or interrupts or degrades a process, even if the system remains available. It is classified by severity S1–S4.
