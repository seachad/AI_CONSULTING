AI action areas

Organisation and people

How an AI-first company is organised and how it attracts and keeps technical talent.

In brief

What this area is about

Useful AI crosses the company's functions, and a function-based organisation chart holds it back. This topic explains which functions someone has to perform, how an AI-first company is organised without one more silo and what changes for people: roles, human oversight and literacy.

Concepts

The concepts you need

Definitions taken from the SEVEN-G glossary (document 02). The codes are links to where each one is explained.

AI-first
Company in which each process is designed on the assumption that AI does the work or proposes the decision, and people set the objective, decide on the exceptions and answer for the result: "AI by default, people where it matters". It is checked with six tests. It does not mean creating an AI function.
End-to-end value chain
Complete process that creates a result for the customer or the company and crosses several functions (for example, order to cash). In an AI-first company it has an owning pair —a business lead and a technology partner with the same objectives— that answers for its result, with a budget in tranches and its own agents. Organisational proposal, not a mandatory body.
AI and data factory
Single unit that brings together the AI teams, the platform, data, model operations and the inventory, and provides that capacity to every value chain with controls built into the platform. It answers for capacity, not for the value of each process. Organisational proposal, not a mandatory body.
Mixed workforce
Workforce made up of people and agents that is planned jointly: each agent has a human owner, appears in the inventory and counts in the workforce plan, with new roles such as agent owner or agent supervisor.
AI Office
Internal methodology, portfolio and measurement unit that maintains the inventory, verifies the gate criteria in Lite-intensity initiatives (in Enterprise-intensity initiatives the AI Auditor verifies them), consolidates measurement and prepares information for the bodies. It must not be confused with the European AI Office.
Human oversight
Design and exercise of what the system decides, what a person validates and what is never delegated, by people with the competence, training and real authority to disagree, correct or interrupt. An overseer without real authority, or assessed on how quickly they accept proposals, does not exercise human oversight.
AI literacy
Sufficient level of AI knowledge among staff and other persons who operate or use AI systems on the company's behalf (EU AI Act, Article 4). In SEVEN-G it is a C2 requirement (corporate policy) and evidence for each initiative (capability plan in P20), organised by profiles (PER-PA to PER-PF, document 50 §5.2).
