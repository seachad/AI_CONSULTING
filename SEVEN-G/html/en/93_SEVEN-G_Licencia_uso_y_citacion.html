        Obligation matrix and layered reading (document 94)

        01Purpose and scope

        This document explains under which licences SEVEN-G is published, what they permit and what they require, how to cite the framework, how to use its name and what happens with derivative works, translations and the fictitious data in the demonstrations, and the scope of the disclaimer and of the user's responsibility for regulatory compliance.

        It applies to all SEVEN-G documents, templates, criteria, measurement rules, graphic components and tools, and to the board dashboard demonstrations built with the methodology.

        07Use of the SEVEN-G name

        7.1 Principle

        The CC BY 4.0 licence permits use of the content; it does not make the user a representative of the framework, nor does it imply endorsement or certification. Naming SEVEN-G to describe accurately what has been used is necessary to comply with the attribution requirement; using it to suggest endorsement, accreditation or certification is not permitted.

        7.2 Appropriate and inappropriate uses

        Appropriate Inappropriate
        "Based on SEVEN-G (version 0.1)." "SEVEN-G certified" or "SEVEN-G certification".
        "Adapted from SEVEN-G (version 0.1); changes: …" "SEVEN-G accredited consultancy", "official partner", "approved".
        "[Company] declares that it applies SEVEN-G (version 0.1)." "Endorsed by Fernando García" or "approved by SEACHAD".
        "Declaration of application reviewed by [third party] on [date]." Presenting as SEVEN-G a service that omits its essential rules without declaring it (document 91, section 2.3).

        7.3 There is no official certification

        In this version there is no official SEVEN-G certification, accreditation or seal. The declaration of application provided for in section 14 of document 01 is a verifiable self-declaration: it is made by the company under its own responsibility and must be demonstrable with evidence. A third party's review of that declaration is a professional opinion of that third party, not a certification, and does not commit the author (document 91, section 6.3).

        08Derivative works and translations

        Scenario Treatment
        Adaptation for internal use Permitted. It is recommended that attribution be kept in the document itself, even if it is not distributed outside the company.
        Adaptation that is shared or published Permitted, with attribution, indication of changes and without suggesting endorsement. Whoever adapts may license their contributions under the terms of their choice, provided that this does not prevent recipients from complying with the licence of the original material. Keeping CC BY 4.0 is recommended to facilitate reuse.
        Official translations The Spanish and English versions of the library are the official ones (decision D12); Spanish is the drafting version.
        Third-party translations Permitted as a derivative work. They must state that they are not official, which version and date they derive from and who produced them.
        Integration into products or tools Permitted. Integrated content retains the attribution; integrated SEVEN-G code retains the MIT notice.
        Works that combine SEVEN-G with other frameworks Permitted. It must be clear which parts come from SEVEN-G.
