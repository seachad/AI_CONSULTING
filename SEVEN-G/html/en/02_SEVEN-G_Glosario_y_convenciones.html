        01Purpose and use of the glossary

        1.1 Purpose

        This document is the single source of SEVEN-G's terms, scales, closed lists, codes and drafting conventions. It brings together the definitions used by the foundational methodology (document 01), the topic documents, templates P01–P31 and tools T01–T22, and sets their official equivalent in English and in Spanish.

        Its purpose is to ensure that a given term means the same thing in a phase manual, in a gate criterion, in a template, in the initiative register and in the board dashboard. Without a common vocabulary it is not possible to aggregate the portfolio, compare periods or audit decisions.

        1.2 Rules of use

        1. Other documents do not redefine. Documents, templates and tools use terms with the meaning given in this glossary. They may develop how they are applied, but they may not alter their definition, values or codes.
        2. The glossary first. If a document needs a new term, a new value in a closed list or a change of definition, this glossary is amended first and the decision is recorded. The affected documents are updated afterwards.
        3. Closed lists. The scales in section 4 and the taxonomy in section 5 are closed lists: tools do not accept any other values. The free tags in the register are the only open space.
        4. Spanish term. The last column gives the original Spanish term, so that the glossary serves as a bridge between the two language versions of the library. The English terms are those of the English translation guide.
        5. Reference document. It indicates where the concept is developed. The binding definition is the one in this glossary; the reference document provides the operational detail.

        1.3 Order of precedence

        If two texts in the library give a term different meanings, the following order applies:

        Order Source Scope
        1 Document 01 · Foundational methodology Normative reference of the framework: model, phases, gates, roles, intensity, risks, nonconformities and regulation.

        Regulatory definitions (EU AI Act, GDPR, DORA, NIS2) are summarised for ease of reading. In case of doubt, the official text of the regulation always prevails, and its validity must be checked (document 34; consulted in September 2026). This document does not constitute legal advice.

        1.4 AI self-consulting principle

        SEVEN-G applies the principle of AI self-consulting: the company must be able to govern, measure and correct its use of AI with its own teams, using a common language and verifiable evidence, without depending on personal interpretations or on a specific supplier.

        This principle exists for four practical reasons:

        1. Continuity. Initiatives last longer than projects and longer than the teams that launched them.
        2. Traceability. The board, audit and risk functions must understand why each step was decided.
        3. Scalability. Without stable definitions it is not possible to compare portfolios, periods and business units.
        4. Transferability. The framework must work even if tools, models or consulting firms change.

        This glossary is therefore not an editorial annex: it is management infrastructure. Autonomy does not mean working without external support; it means that control of the method and of the evidence stays within the company.

        In line with this principle, SEVEN-G is compatible with in-house or external consulting support and with other corporate frameworks for governance, risk, technology or transformation. It may be applied in full or in a modular way, provided its rules for traceability, verification and accountability are maintained.

        1.5 Didactic, but not a training course

        SEVEN-G documents are final management documents, not course material. They must be didactic to read, so as to make correct decisions easier, according to three criteria:

        1. What it is. A precise, single definition.
        2. Why it matters. Its relationship with value, risk, compliance or decision-making.
        3. How it is used. The evidence, gate criterion or tool where it applies.

        Tools follow the same logic: they do not teach theory; they guide the application of the framework with closed lists, validations and evidence records.

        From this version onward, whenever a new concept is introduced (including a template, document or tool), it must include an explanatory zone labeled "Why it matters", explicitly stating its practical relevance for decision-making, risk, compliance or measurement.

        02Normative language and drafting conventions

        2.1 Normative language

        Term Meaning Consequence Spanish
        must Mandatory requirement. Failure to comply is a nonconformity (section 4.13). debe
        should Recommendation. May be omitted if justified and recorded. Omitting it without justification is an audit observation. debería
        may Permitted option. No justification required. puede

        Negative forms follow the same logic: must not prohibits; should not advises against, with the possibility of justification. Expressions such as "it is advisable", "it is strongly recommended" or "it is necessary" are not used in normative text, because they do not make clear whether there is an obligation.

        2.2 Drafting

        • Language and tone. Spanish from Spain in the original version and international English with British spelling in the English version; formal, clear and without a commercial tone. Short sentences; tables where they organise information better than text.
        • Capitals. Values of scales and closed lists take an initial capital when used as such: outcomes (Proceed, Pivot), ambition levels (Optimise), statuses (Met, In production), grades (Systematic), risk levels (High). In the Spanish version, roles and bodies are written in lower case in running text and with an initial capital in headings, tables and records; the English version keeps the role and body names of the translation guide (AI Committee, AI Risk Owner).
        • Anglicisms. Gate is used in italics as a synonym for decision gate. In the Spanish version, other anglicisms are used only when there is no established equivalent and are shown in italics the first time they appear (shadow AI).
        • Acronyms. They are spelled out the first time they appear in each document, except those in section 8 in general use in the framework (AI, GDPR, EU).
        • Examples. Any example, case or figure that does not come from a regulation or a verifiable source is marked as illustrative or fictitious. No client data or recognisable client features are used.
        • Figures. No market figures, improvement percentages or return references are included without a verifiable source. The framework's thresholds are presented as indicative or to be calibrated and are set by each company in C2.
        • Regulations. Only articles and requirements that exist are cited, with the date of consultation when there are recent changes or changes in progress. Every document with regulatory content includes the disclaimer: "This document does not constitute legal advice."
        • Diagrams. Diagrams are not drawn with text characters. The framework's graphic components or Mermaid diagrams are used.
        • Brands. SEVEN-G is the only framework. SPAD and AECF are cited as related methodologies, not as parallel brands.

        2.5 "No data"

        An absent value is written as "no data", never as zero or as an undeclared estimate (measurement rule 8). In scales that score, "no data" scores 0 and is shown as "no data"; in traffic lights it is shown in grey and never as green.
