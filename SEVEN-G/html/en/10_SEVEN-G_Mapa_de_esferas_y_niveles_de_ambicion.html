        Obligation matrix and layered reading (document 94)

        01Purpose and scope

        This document develops the SEVEN-G impact map: nine spheres in which artificial intelligence affects an organisation and three ambition levels at which each of them can be addressed. It establishes:

        • What each sphere covers, what questions the board should ask, which examples correspond to each level and which signs indicate that something is not going well.
        • Indicators with a formula for each sphere, so that the map can be measured and not merely described.
        • The classification rules for placing an initiative on the map: primary sphere, secondary sphere and ambition level.
        • The specific rule for spheres 08 and 09, which are assessed on their own levels and must not be mixed with Optimise, Augment and Transform.
        • The portfolio heat map (tool T16) and its use in the corporate cycle (C1 to C5) and in the lifecycle (phases 1, 2 and 7).

        The detailed classification of an initiative's ambition level (five questions and decision rules) is in document 12. Setting the target ambition by sphere is an output of C2 and is documented with document 13.

        The indicator codes in this document (IE01.01…) are provisional. Document 41 (Indicator catalogue) consolidates them with the rest of the framework's indicators.

        02The impact map

        A board does not decide on models or platforms: it decides on risk, opportunity, competitive advantage and accountability. The map makes it possible to talk about AI without talking about technology and to check that the company governs it across the organisation, not as a set of isolated technical projects.

        Type Sphere Motto What it contributes to the analysis
        Where value is created 01 · Customer Knowledge, relationship and anticipation Revenue, retention and experience.
        02 · Product and service AI inside, on top of or as the product Offering, differentiation and new revenue.
        03 · People Recipients, subjects and promoters of AI Work, capabilities, roles and organisation.
        04 · Operations Processes, value chain and efficiency Cost, time, quality and operational resilience.
        Enablers 05 · Data The material substrate of all AI Availability, quality and legitimate use of data.
        06 · Knowledge Organisational memory and collective intelligence Critical knowledge that is accessible and not dependent on a few people.
        07 · Decision Who decides, with what information and with how much delegation Allocation of decisions between people and systems.
        Boundaries 08 · Regulation, ethics and accountability Regulation, principles and chain of accountability Compliance, anticipation and ethical leadership.
        Meta-sphere 09 · AI governance The sphere that connects the other eight Structure, balance between speed and control, and supplier ecosystem.

        Principles for using the map:

        1. The nine spheres cover the whole organisation. If an initiative does not fit into any of them, its business objective is most probably unclear.
        2. The enabling spheres constrain the value spheres. An ambition to Transform in Customer or in Product without sufficient capability in Data, Knowledge and Decision is a sign of risk, not of boldness.
        3. Spheres 08 and 09 do not generate value in themselves: they set the boundaries and the governance system. That is why they are assessed on their own levels (sections 6 and 7).
        4. The map is read at three points in time: current situation (C1), target ambition (C2) and the portfolio that delivers it (C3).

        03The three ambition levels

        Ambition levels are not a ladder. It is not mandatory to go through Optimise to reach Transform, nor is Transform better than Optimise. They are three types of bet with different cost, risk, timescale and organisational resistance, which are chosen deliberately.

        Aspect Optimise Augment Transform
        What it means Doing the same thing faster, more cheaply or with fewer errors. People do what they could not do before. What is offered, how the company competes or how it is organised changes.
        What changes The efficiency of a task or process. What is done and who does it do not change. The role and capabilities of people. The business does not change. The value proposition, the revenue model or the operating model.
        Where the value lies Mainly in efficiencies (avoided cost). Combined: cost and performance (productivity, quality, conversion). Mainly in return and in changes to the operating model.
        Uncertainty Low: the baseline is known. Medium: it depends on adoption. High: it depends on the market, the customer or the organisation.
        Organisational resistance Low or localised. Medium: it requires a change of habits and role. High: it affects structures, incentives and decision-making power.
        Main gate criterion Positive annual net value within the horizon set in C2. Actual adoption and performance improvement. Learning milestones, investment limit per stage and market evidence.
        Who decides Area management, within the portfolio framework. Management, with visibility for the AI Committee. Explicit decision of the board or senior management.
        Common mistake Counting released capacity as savings. Measuring assigned licences instead of usage and performance. Calling an automation with a good narrative a transformation.

        General rules:

        • Every initiative has a single ambition level, classified with the five questions in document 12. If an initiative combines components of different levels, it is split or classified according to the component that justifies the investment.
        • Ambition refers to what changes in the business, not to the technology used. A generative AI agent can be Optimise; a classical statistical model can be Transform.
        • The ambition of a sphere (in C1 or in C2) is expressed through the levels of the initiatives that make it up; it is not assigned by opinion.

        04Classification rules on the map

        4.1 Primary sphere and secondary sphere

        Each initiative is recorded in the initiative register (T01) with a mandatory primary sphere and, at most, one secondary sphere.

        # Rule
        1 The primary sphere is the one in which the main metric of the value hypothesis is measured. It is not decided by the technology, by the sponsoring area or by the supplier.
        2 The secondary sphere is only recorded if there is a second relevant effect measurable with an indicator of that sphere or an explicit organisational change. It is not used to flag generic dependencies.
        3 Spheres 05 Data, 06 Knowledge and 07 Decision are primary only when the main outcome is the enabling capability itself (a data catalogue, a knowledge base, a decision system). The fact that an initiative uses data does not place it in sphere 05.
        4 Sphere 03 People is primary when the main outcome concerns work, capabilities or the organisation. It should be recorded as secondary in every Augment or Transform initiative that changes roles.
        5 Spheres 08 and 09 are primary only when the purpose of the initiative is compliance or governance (for example, an AI system to monitor regulatory obligations or to maintain the inventory). They are never used to indicate that an initiative carries regulatory risk: that is recorded in the regulatory classification and in the risk register.
        6 Investment and value are allocated only to the primary sphere (measurement rule 5: each euro is attributed to a single use case). The secondary sphere appears on the map as a marker, without amounts.
        7 If the main metric changes at G2 or G7, the primary sphere is reviewed and the change is recorded as an event.

        Common borderline cases (illustrative examples):

        Initiative Primary Secondary Reason
        Assistant that drafts responses to customer complaints for the customer service team 04 Operations 01 Customer, only if satisfaction or repeat contact is measured The main metric is handling time per complaint.
        Product recommender in the digital channel 01 Customer 02 Product and service, if it modifies the offering The main metric is incremental conversion.
        Common data platform for several AI use cases 05 Data — The outcome is the enabling capability; the value of the use cases that use it is allocated to those use cases.
        Generative assistant built into the office suite for several business units 03 People 06 Knowledge, if access to information is measured It is a cross-unit initiative: it is recorded once and measured by business unit (document 40 §7.2). It is not an enabling capability of spheres 05 to 07: its outcome falls on people's work.
        Copilot for risk analysts that enables them to review more complex transactions 03 People or 07 Decision The other of the two Primary according to the metric: analyst performance (03) or decision quality (07).
        AI system that monitors regulatory changes and assigns them to owners 08 Regulation, ethics and accountability — Its purpose is regulatory anticipation.

        4.2 Rule for spheres 08 and 09

        Spheres 08 and 09 are not assessed with Optimise, Augment and Transform, because they do not generate value in themselves. They have their own dimensions and grades:

        Sphere Dimensions Grades for each dimension
        08 · Regulation, ethics and accountability Compliance · Anticipation · Ethical leadership Absent · Basic · Systematic · Advanced
        09 · AI governance Structure · Balance between speed and control · Supplier ecosystem Absent · Basic · Systematic · Advanced

        To avoid mixing them with the three levels, the following rules apply:

        # Rule Mistake it prevents
        1 On the heat map, rows 08 and 09 show grades by dimension, never Optimise, Augment or Transform columns. Presenting "Transform in Regulation" as if it were a business bet.
        2 The C2 target ambition for 08 and 09 is expressed as a target grade by dimension (document 13). Setting "Augment governance" as a target, which is not verifiable.
        3 An initiative whose primary sphere is 08 or 09 has an ambition level (it is classified with the five questions, like all the others), but that level is not used to rate the sphere. Its contribution is also recorded as the dimension it contributes to (for example, Anticipation). Confusing the type of bet of a tool with the company's grade of governance.
        4 The investment of initiatives whose primary sphere is 08 or 09 is shown in a separate band of the heat map ("governance and compliance enablement") and is excluded from signals 1, 2 and 7 of the transformation index (document 12). Investment in governance being read as "efficiency" and lowering the profile, or being used to inflate ambition.
        5 The grades of 08 and 09 are not maturity levels. They serve as evidence for dimensions D6 (Risk, security and compliance) and D1 (Strategy and governance) of document 11, but do not replace them. Duplicating or contradicting the maturity diagnosis.
        6 Spheres 08 and 09 cannot be the secondary sphere of an initiative whose purpose is not compliance or governance. Using the 08 label as a synonym for "carries regulatory risk".

        Common definition of the grades:

        Grade General criterion Minimum evidence
        Absent There is no defined owner or practice, or they exist only on an ad hoc and undocumented basis. —
        Basic Practices and owners exist, but they depend on specific people or projects and do not cover the whole scope. Partial documents or records.
        Systematic Defined, approved process applied to the whole scope, with owners and verifiable evidence. Approved procedure, complete records, calculated indicators.
        Advanced Systematic, with indicators on target for at least two quarters, continuous improvement and review by the third line. Internal or external audit report with no open major nonconformities.

        05Profiles of spheres 01 to 07

        Each profile has the same structure: what it covers, questions for the board, examples by level, warning signs and indicators. The examples are generic and illustrative; they are neither recommendations nor market references. The indicators are calculated over the stated scope (company, unit or initiative) and follow the measurement rules in document 00: every amount has a formula and a status, and "no data" is not zero.

        5.1 Sphere 01 · Customer

        What it covers. Customer knowledge, segmentation, acquisition, relationship, service, retention and anticipation of needs, across all channels. It does not cover the design of the offering (sphere 02) or the internal efficiency of the customer service back office (sphere 04), unless the main metric relates to the customer.

        Questions for the board

        • If a competitor knows our customers better because it uses AI better, how long will it take us to lose relevance?
        • What part of the customer relationship is already mediated by AI systems, and who is accountable for its quality?
        • Do we know whether AI improves retention and conversion, or whether it only reduces the cost of serving?
        • What information do we give customers when they interact with an AI system?

        Examples by level (illustrative)

        Level Examples What distinguishes it
        Optimise Behaviour-based segmentation instead of static demographic data · Classification and routing of enquiries · Early detection of churn risk for existing campaigns. Customer service is the same; its cost or accuracy improves.
        Augment Real-time personalisation of the proposition · Conversational assistants that resolve complete enquiries with controlled handover · Sentiment analysis across all channels to anticipate crises. Sales and customer service teams do what they could not do before.
        Transform An AI agent becomes the main point of contact · Discovery of unserved market segments with a dedicated offering · Product co-creation with the customer mediated by generative AI. How the company relates to customers, or whom it targets, changes.

        Warning signs

        • The assistant's containment rate is measured, but repeat contact and satisfaction are not.
        • There is no control group to attribute retention or conversion to AI.
        • Complaints related to automated responses increase and nobody analyses them separately.
        • Customers do not know they are interacting with an AI system when regulation requires that they be informed.

        Indicators

        Code Indicator Formula Source
        IE01.01 AI coverage of the customer relationship Interactions handled or assisted by AI ÷ total interactions × 100 Customer service systems and channels
        IE01.02 Effective resolution without handover Interactions resolved by AI with no handover or repeat contact within 7 days ÷ interactions handled by AI × 100 Customer service systems
        IE01.03 Satisfaction differential Average satisfaction in interactions with AI − average satisfaction without AI (same scale and period) Satisfaction surveys
        IE01.04 Incremental retention in monetary terms (Retention rate of the treated group − rate of the control group) × customers treated × average annual margin per customer T12, management control
        IE01.05 Incremental conversion in monetary terms (Conversion with AI − conversion of the control group) × opportunities treated × average margin per sale T12, sales CRM
        IE01.06 Complaints attributable to AI Complaints whose recorded cause is an AI system ÷ interactions with AI × 10,000 Complaints register

        5.2 Sphere 02 · Product and service

        What it covers. AI embedded in the product or service, AI that speeds up its design and launch, and AI that is the product itself. It does not cover the commercial relationship with the customer (sphere 01).

        Questions for the board

        • Are we using AI to protect the current product or to build the next one? If both strategies compete for the same resources, who decides?
        • What part of our revenue comes from products or services that would not exist without AI?
        • Do we know the AI cost per unit of service and its effect on margin?
        • What accountability do we assume towards customers for the behaviour of the AI included in our product?

        Examples by level (illustrative)

        Level Examples What distinguishes it
        Optimise Automated testing and defect detection · Assisted generation of code, design or copy to reduce time to launch · Product tuning based on actual usage. The product is the same; it is produced or improved at lower cost or in less time.
        Augment Intelligent features within the product (recommendations, predictions, assistants) · Product that adapts to each user's context · Dynamically generated documentation and onboarding. The product offers users new capabilities without changing the business model.
        Transform Service that exists only because there is a model behind it · Shift from selling a product to selling an ongoing capability · Modular offering of capabilities orchestrated by AI. What is sold and how it is charged for changes.

        Warning signs

        • AI features are announced without measuring their actual usage.
        • The AI cost per unit of service is unknown or grows faster than the product's revenue.
        • There is no product owner accountable to the customer for the behaviour of the AI.
        • New product initiatives are assessed with the same payback period as efficiency initiatives and are stopped before market evidence is obtained.

        Indicators

        Code Indicator Formula Source
        IE02.01 AI-enabled revenue Revenue from products, services or channels that would not exist without AI ÷ total revenue × 100 (same definition as signal 6 in document 12) Management control, T12
        IE02.02 Use of AI features Active users who use AI features in the period ÷ active users of the product × 100 Product telemetry
        IE02.03 Reduction in time to launch (Median days from idea to launch in the baseline − current median) ÷ baseline median × 100 Product management
        IE02.04 AI cost per unit of service Recurring AI cost allocated to the product ÷ units of service delivered T13, document 42
        IE02.05 Margin of the AI-enabled offering (Revenue from the AI-enabled offering − direct costs, including allocated AI cost) ÷ revenue from the AI-enabled offering × 100 Management control
        IE02.06 Defects contained before launch Defects detected in testing ÷ (defects detected in testing + defects detected in production) × 100 Quality management

        5.3 Sphere 03 · People

        What it covers. The effect of AI on work, capabilities, roles, organisational structure and the relationship with employees. People play three simultaneous roles with regard to AI:

        Role What it means What it requires of the company
        Recipients AI changes their tools, processes and productivity expectations, without them having chosen it. Change management, training and AI literacy; adoption depends on them.
        Subjects AI can restructure, reassign or eliminate jobs. Explicit decision by management and the board, dialogue with employee representatives where appropriate, and responsible treatment.
        Promoters Those who know a process best can identify uses of AI that no outsider would see. A channel for proposing initiatives that enter the register and the portfolio.

        Questions for the board

        • Does AI replace, augment or reorganise people's work? That answer defines the culture, the employer brand and talent retention.
        • What happens to released capacity: does it become lower cost, is it reassigned or does it simply disappear?
        • Do the people who use or oversee AI have training appropriate to their role?
        • What proportion of initiatives originates from employees themselves?

        Examples by level (illustrative)

        Level Examples What distinguishes it
        Optimise Automation of repetitive tasks (reports, data entry, triage) · Accelerated onboarding with knowledge assistants · Training adapted to each employee's level. The job is the same; low-value work is eliminated.
        Augment Specialised copilot by function · Employees who propose and deploy uses of AI in their area under control · Continuous reskilling so that the role evolves with AI. People carry out tasks they could not do before; the role changes.
        Transform Reorganisation of teams around AI systems · Disappearance of functions and creation of new roles · People who oversee autonomous systems instead of executing. The organisational structure and the division of work change.

        Warning signs

        • Released hours are reported without stating whether they have been realised or reassigned.
        • The number of assigned licences is measured, not actual usage.
        • Decisions on jobs are taken as a technical consequence of a project, without an explicit management decision.
        • There is no adoption plan in Augment or Transform initiatives.
        • Turnover in the affected groups rises without anyone analysing it.

        Indicators

        Code Indicator Formula Source
        IE03.01 Effective adoption Users with active usage in the period (threshold of days of use set by the company) ÷ users with access × 100 Telemetry, T21
        IE03.02 Realised released capacity Released hours converted into actual lower cost ÷ measured released hours × 100 T12, T20
        IE03.03 Reassigned released capacity Hours explicitly reassigned to recorded activities ÷ measured released hours × 100 T20
        IE03.04 AI literacy by role People with accredited AI training appropriate to their role ÷ people who use, oversee or make decisions on AI × 100 Training register
        IE03.05 Redesigned roles Jobs with a description updated as a result of AI ÷ jobs affected by Augment or Transform initiatives × 100 People, T20
        IE03.06 Employee-promoted initiatives Employee-originated initiatives that pass G1 ÷ employee-originated initiatives registered × 100 T01 (origin field)

        5.4 Sphere 04 · Operations

        What it covers. Internal processes, supply chain, production, maintenance, back office, fraud control and operational risks. It does not cover processes whose main metric relates to the customer (sphere 01).

        Questions for the board

        • Which processes have a demonstrable return within the horizon set in C2, and which require a complete redesign?
        • Do we know which are candidates for immediate automation? If we do not, that is the first piece of work.
        • Are we automating tasks or redesigning end-to-end processes?
        • What happens if an AI system supporting a critical operation fails or degrades?

        Examples by level (illustrative)

        Level Examples What distinguishes it
        Optimise Document processing and reconciliations · Demand forecasting and inventory optimisation · Predictive maintenance · Fraud and anomaly detection. The process is the same; its cost, time or error rate improves.
        Augment Supply chain that adjusts to market signals · Planning with simulated scenarios · Quality control with computer vision or language processing. Operational teams make decisions with capabilities they did not have.
        Transform Processes that run and correct themselves with aggregate oversight · Orchestration of workflows by multi-agent systems · Elimination of entire links in the value chain. The process design and the operating model change.

        Warning signs

        • Declared savings with no actual cost reduction in the income statement.
        • Isolated task automations that shift the bottleneck to the next step.
        • Critical processes supported by AI without a tested rollback plan.
        • Agents that execute operational actions without a defined autonomy level.

        Indicators

        Code Indicator Formula Source
        IE04.01 Cycle time reduction (Baseline cycle time − current cycle time) ÷ baseline cycle time × 100 Process systems
        IE04.02 Change in unit cost (Total process cost ÷ units processed) − baseline unit cost Management control
        IE04.03 Straight-through processing Transactions completed without manual intervention ÷ total transactions × 100 Process systems
        IE04.04 Error or rework rate Units with errors or rework ÷ units processed × 100, compared with the baseline Quality management
        IE04.05 Processes redesigned end to end AI-enabled processes redesigned end to end and verified at G5 or G7 ÷ AI-enabled processes in production × 100 T01 (question 2 verified)
        IE04.06 Validated realised savings Σ efficiencies with validated status and an effect on actual cost in the period (€) T12

        5.5 Sphere 05 · Data

        What it covers. Availability, quality, cataloguing, lineage, legal basis and architecture of the data used by AI systems, and data as a product. Document 51 develops its governance.

        Questions for the board

        • Do we know what data we have, where it is, who is accountable for its quality and whether we can legally use it for AI?
        • How many initiatives are delayed or stopped for lack of data?
        • Have we documented where the data used by each system in production to make decisions comes from?
        • Is our data an advantage that a competitor cannot replicate?

        Examples by level (illustrative)

        Level Examples What distinguishes it
        Optimise Cleansing, standardisation and deduplication · Cataloguing with AI-generated metadata and automated lineage · Continuous monitoring of quality and drift. Existing data is managed better and with less effort.
        Augment Synthetic data where real data is scarce · Enrichment with external sources · Natural-language data queries by any authorised employee. More people can use more data to make decisions.
        Transform Data turned into a product or service · Distributed, domain-governed data architecture · Living representation of operations fed in real time. Data changes the business model or the operating model.

        Warning signs

        • Initiatives that reach phase 3 without having confirmed access to the data.
        • Personal data used to train or feed systems without a documented legal basis.
        • Critical datasets without an identified owner.
        • Systems in production without data and model lineage.

        Indicators

        Code Indicator Formula Source
        IE05.01 Catalogue coverage Datasets used by AI systems in production that are catalogued and have an owner ÷ datasets used × 100 Data catalogue, T02
        IE05.02 Quality of critical data Critical datasets that meet the defined quality thresholds ÷ critical datasets × 100 Quality monitoring
        IE05.03 Documented legal basis Datasets with personal data used in AI with a documented legal basis ÷ datasets with personal data used in AI × 100 Data protection
        IE05.04 Documented lineage AI systems in production with verified data and model lineage (P16) ÷ AI systems in production × 100 T02, P16
        IE05.05 Time to data access Median days between an initiative's data request and its availability in the authorised environment T01 (events)
        IE05.06 Initiatives blocked by data Initiatives stopped with the reason "Insufficient data" or on hold for data ÷ initiatives stopped or on hold in the period × 100 T01

        5.6 Sphere 06 · Knowledge

        What it covers. Documented and tacit knowledge, organisational memory, knowledge search and reuse, domain-specialised assistants and organisational learning. Document 51 develops its management.

        Questions for the board

        • How much critical knowledge depends on a few people and is not available to the organisation?
        • If the people who know the most left tomorrow, what would be lost? AI can narrow that gap, but only if work begins before they leave.
        • Are the answers from our knowledge assistants reliable, and who verifies them?
        • Do we learn from what we have decided and done, or do we repeat mistakes?

        Examples by level (illustrative)

        Level Examples What distinguishes it
        Optimise Semantic search over internal documentation · FAQ bases updated with AI · Meeting transcription and summarisation with a record of decisions. What was already written is found sooner.
        Augment Capture of experts' tacit knowledge · Domain knowledge assistants (legal, technical, regulatory, product) · Detection of patterns of success and failure in previous projects. People access expert knowledge they did not have before.
        Transform The organisation's knowledge as an active system that proposes and does not merely respond · Proprietary knowledge as a competitive advantage that is hard to replicate · The company retains what it has done, decided and learned. Knowledge changes how the company competes or is organised.

        Warning signs

        • Knowledge assistants without an assessment of the accuracy of their answers.
        • Outdated documentation feeding generative AI systems.
        • Critical domains that depend on one or two people without a capture plan.
        • Access to confidential information through assistants without permission controls.

        Indicators

        Code Indicator Formula Source
        IE06.01 Coverage of critical knowledge Critical domains with documented and accessible knowledge ÷ critical domains identified × 100 Knowledge map
        IE06.02 Knowledge concentration Critical domains that depend on two people or fewer ÷ critical domains identified × 100 Knowledge map
        IE06.03 Verified accuracy Correct answers in a sample reviewed by experts ÷ answers reviewed × 100 Assistant evaluations
        IE06.04 Useful resolution Queries marked as resolved by the user and confirmed by sampling ÷ total queries × 100 Assistant telemetry
        IE06.05 Currency of sources Documents feeding AI systems reviewed within the set time limit ÷ documents feeding AI systems × 100 Document management
        IE06.06 Time to autonomy Median days until a new joiner reaches autonomy in their role, compared with the baseline People

        5.7 Sphere 07 · Decision

        What it covers. Which decisions AI systems make, recommend or execute, with what information, at what autonomy level (A0 to A3) and with what human oversight and traceability.

        Questions for the board

        • What does AI decide on its own, what requires human validation and what is never delegated? If we have not defined this, we are delegating by default.
        • Can we reconstruct what the system recommended and what the person decided in each relevant decision?
        • Which systems act with A2 or A3 autonomy, and who can stop them?
        • Are decisions improving with AI, or are they just being made faster?

        Examples by level (illustrative)

        Level Examples What distinguishes it
        Optimise Dashboards that prioritise relevant information · Predictive alerts before an indicator deteriorates · Automation of low-risk, high-frequency decisions. The same decisions, with less effort or sooner.
        Augment Options with risk and probability analysis from which a person chooses · Scenario simulation · Traceability of what AI recommended and what the person decided. Decision-makers have analytical capabilities they did not have.
        Transform Autonomous decisions in bounded domains (dynamic pricing, resource allocation) · Coordination of decisions between systems with aggregate oversight · Executives who oversee decision systems instead of deciding each case. Who decides and how decision-making is organised change.

        Warning signs

        • Systems that execute actions without an assigned autonomy level or a kill switch.
        • A human override rate close to zero for months: it may indicate nominal oversight.
        • A very high override rate: the system is not calibrated or is not trusted.
        • Decisions with significant effects on people without meaningful human review.

        Indicators

        Code Indicator Formula Source
        IE07.01 Decisions with assigned autonomy Decision types in which AI is involved with an assigned and documented A0–A3 level ÷ decision types in which AI is involved × 100 T02, P17
        IE07.02 Decision traceability Assisted decisions with a record of the recommendation and of the human decision ÷ assisted decisions × 100 System logs
        IE07.03 Human override rate Recommendations overridden or modified by the person ÷ recommendations issued × 100 (interpreted against a band set by the AI Product Owner) System logs
        IE07.04 Tested kill switch A2 or A3 systems with a kill switch tested in the last 12 months ÷ A2 or A3 systems × 100 T10
        IE07.05 Improvement in decision quality Average outcome of decisions with AI − average outcome of the control group, converted into money (for example, loss avoided) T12
        IE07.06 Decision latency Median time from when the information is available until the decision is executed, compared with the baseline Process logs

        06Sphere 08 · Regulation, ethics and accountability

        What it covers. The regulatory framework applicable to AI, the company's voluntary ethical principles and the chain of accountability when something goes wrong. The detail of obligations is in document 34 and the corporate policy in document 31. This document does not constitute legal advice.

        Reference question for the board: do we govern proactively or do we wait for the regulator to force us? The cost of the second option is measured in penalties, but above all in reputation and trust.

        6.1 Dimensions and grades

        Dimension What it assesses Basic Systematic Advanced
        Compliance Classification of systems under the EU AI Act, transparency and oversight obligations, data protection in automated decision-making and profiling, sector regulation. Classification and assessments for some systems, on demand. All inventoried systems are classified; the required assessments are carried out before G3; nonconformities are managed with time limits. Systematic, with indicators on target for two quarters and an audit with no open major nonconformities.
        Anticipation Monitoring of emerging regulation, impact assessments before deployment and preparation for external audits. Regulation is followed informally. Monitoring process with an owner; each relevant change has an impact analysis within the time limit; mock audits. The company adapts its systems before the obligation becomes applicable and documents this.
        Ethical leadership Own principles beyond the legal minimum, an ethics body with real veto power and public transparency about the use of AI. Published principles with no enforcement mechanism. Principles approved by the board, ethical review of the initiatives that require it and a body with documented veto power. Periodic public report on the use of AI and recorded veto or modification decisions.

        6.2 Questions for the board by dimension

        Dimension Questions

        07Sphere 09 · AI governance

        What it covers. The meta-sphere that connects the other eight: ultimate accountability for AI, bodies, portfolio, gates, reporting to the board, balance between speed and control, and the relationship with AI suppliers. Document 30 develops the governance model and document 36 third-party management.

        Reference question for the board: does AI have corporate governance with the same formality as finance, risk or compliance?

        7.1 Dimensions and grades

        Dimension Questions for the board Basic Systematic Advanced
        Structure Who has ultimate accountability for the AI strategy? Is there a framework that connects it with the business strategy? Is there a portfolio prioritised by impact and risk? Does the board receive regular and understandable information? Designated owner; portfolio as a list of projects. AI thesis and risk appetite approved; AI Committee operational; initiative register with roles and gates; quarterly dashboard to the board. Systematic, with the register of recommendations up to date and the annual C5 review completed.
        Balance between speed and control Are we accelerating adoption with risk under control? Are there decision gates in the lifecycle? How do we measure our maturity? Can we stop an initiative that is not working? Some controls, applied unevenly. Gates with dual validation in all initiatives; Lite or Enterprise intensity determined; reference time limits measured; stops and retirements recorded. Decision times within time limits without an increase in nonconformities; annual calibration of time limits and thresholds.
        Supplier ecosystem Do we depend on a single model supplier? Do we assess build, buy or partner for each capability? Do contracts protect our data and intellectual property? Do we understand the cost structure of AI? Known suppliers, without consistent assessment. Supplier register with N1–N3 requirement level; minimum contractual clauses; cost by category and by use case. Tested exit strategy for N3 suppliers and concentration within risk appetite.

        • Initiatives in production without a recorded gate or without a current continuity review.

        • A single supplier accounts for most of the spending on models without an exit strategy.

        Scope Active initiatives (from G0 passed until retirement) and those stopped or retired in the last 12 months, which are shown separately.
